# Lexity Conversion 2.0

Reassessed sprint plan for Rexity Labs · 22 September 2026

Status: planning document for independent review. No website redesign, marketing campaign, CRM integration, or indexing submission was deployed in this reassessment. “Lexity Conversion 2.0” is the requested initiative name; the public company remains Rexity Labs.

## 1. Decision

Build a conventional, evidence-led agency acquisition system: focused services, real case studies, useful buyer content, referrals, a short project enquiry, and human qualification. Make UX the primary offer and implementation expertise the supporting reason to buy.

Proposed positioning: “Rexity designs and builds usable websites, apps, and business tools.” Explain the specific customer journey and the operational system behind it through actual projects.

This plan uses established practices with documented precedents. It does not claim that another company's results prove the same results for Rexity. Public agency examples are observational or self-reported, not controlled experiments. Local demand, pricing, conversion rates, and sales timing remain unverified. Measuring implementation and market fit is necessary even when the method is established.

## 5. Audience and commercial boundaries

Primary buyer: a founder or business owner who needs a clearer customer or operator journey, with the option of having Rexity implement it.

Initial project types: booking/enquiry websites and product/dashboard UX. Relevant existing proof: Body & Care, Chara and LevelKraft. RexDesk and the second product can strengthen operational UX proof once their capabilities are verified.

Geography: Jammu and Delhi NCR are requested audiences. The Rajasthan location is unresolved; Alwar–Bhiwadi–Neemrana was only a previous assumption. Do not publish that assumption as an office, service promise or market fact.

Before pricing or regional copy is final, the founder supplies:

1. Minimum project value, available delivery hours and sustainable response coverage.
2. Exact Rajasthan audience, real working locations, and any in-person arrangements.
3. Confirmed public spelling and capabilities of RexFanks/RexFangs.
4. Permission to use each client's name, screens, testimonial and business measurements.
5. Verified scope and dependencies behind local hosting, data ownership, RFQ, planning, chat and assistant claims.

These do not block technical audits or drafting. They gate only dependent publishing, pricing and claims.

Offers: scoped UX review; UX design engagement; design-and-build engagement. Review and design scopes specify journeys/screens, research access, outputs, revisions, exclusions and handoff. The first response qualifies the project; a full bespoke audit is paid work. Prices and turnaround are costed in Sprint 1, not copied from another studio.

## 6. Website specification

Keep the existing German root and useful URLs. Build `/en/` as the English home, with English offers, work, founder and contact pages. Preserve existing project URLs; create English equivalents only when fully translated. Use self-canonicals and reciprocal language links for equivalent pages. Do not redirect visitors based on inferred country.

Proposed English navigation: Work · Services · About · Insights · Discuss a project.

Homepage sequence:

1. Specific UX/design-to-build offer, real screenshot, primary enquiry CTA and secondary work CTA.
2. Three selected projects, each with problem, Rexity's role and case-study link.
3. Three engagement options with concrete deliverables.
4. Verified in-house products, clearly labeled separately from client work.
5. One implemented workflow showing how the interface connects to operations.
6. Founder profile and attributable feedback, where available.
7. Two useful articles and a short enquiry path.

The proposed headline is a draft: “UX design and software development for websites, apps, and business tools.” Supporting copy should identify the users and work Rexity actually performs. Technical depth belongs in project explanations. Avoid a homepage list of every technology and automation feature.

Design direction: readable typography, real interface screens, consistent navigation, modest motion, clear focus states and mobile usability. This is an implementation brief, not an experimentally validated layout. Audit the existing stack before choosing changes; a framework rewrite is not a prerequisite.

Essential content and internal links must render in the main HTML. Keep illustrations/animations optional. Remove unrelated template text, stale slot counts and misleading labels. Use approved snippets and keep chatbot boilerplate out of snippets using the appropriate supported markup. Do not describe stack vendors as partners unless that relationship exists.

## 8. Distribution and conversion

Priority order:

1. Existing-client and trusted-contact referrals, supported by a relevant case-study link [E1, E2]. Plan permission-based introductions; no cold calling, scraped lists or unsolicited automated sequences. No messages are authorized by this document alone.
2. Useful, buyer-oriented articles with links to work and services [E3]. Interviews with the founder and project records supply substance.
3. Founder posts distributing those same lessons; proposed capacity limit of two posts weekly. This cadence is a work budget, not a proven conversion formula.
4. Accurate profiles and local/community participation only where appropriate. Google Business Profile is conditional on eligibility [E10]. Broader regional expansion follows evidence, not a city-page quota.

Buyer path: referral/search/post → relevant case study or article → related offer → project brief → human qualification → discovery/proposal → project delivery.

Form: name, email, short problem description; website/product link, timeframe and budget band can be optional, including “not sure”. No compulsory phone number or account creation. No confidential uploads in the initial form. Explain what happens next.

Use the existing inbox/CRM if reliable. Reuse RexDesk only after confirming capability and effort. Create no new CRM platform for this launch. Store source page, available campaign/referrer information, self-reported source and stage. Avoid collecting sensitive information in analytics events.

Stages: new → reviewed → qualified → discovery → proposal → won/lost, with an owner and next action. Set a response target the founder can honor. Optional booking follows the brief; email remains usable. Test confirmation, internal delivery, failure feedback, spam handling and duplicates. Transactional acknowledgements are distinct from marketing subscriptions.

A lead is qualified when the need fits, a relevant decision-maker is reachable, commercial scope is plausible and delivery capacity exists. Report reasons for disqualification. Warm referral work and inbound sales conversations remain necessary even without cold calling.

## 10. Verification and decision rules

Technical: status and redirects, crawl directives, unique metadata, language equivalents, sitemap URLs, main-document content, relevant internal links, real 404s, broken assets, keyboard/mobile navigation and form delivery. Inspect applicable structured data; do not add schema as a ranking promise. Establish a repeatable mobile performance baseline before setting a performance-improvement claim.

Usability: ask a small set of representative buyers to locate relevant work, explain the offer and submit a sample enquiry. This detects problems; it does not establish statistically general conversion uplift. Capture objections and fix clear defects.

Report weekly: non-brand search impressions/clicks; relevant landing visits by source; valid submissions; qualified leads; proposals; wins; actual revenue where available; founder/content effort. Separate internal/test traffic where possible. Store self-reported source because referrers and campaign tags can be missing.

Compare equal date windows and annotate releases. Search Console “three months” is not necessarily three months of available data. Do not interpret a blended average position or a tiny CTR change as proof of local ranking improvement. SEO growth cannot be assigned to the redesign alone if distribution and content changed at the same time.

Commercial planning uses: required qualified opportunities = desired wins / assumed close rate. For illustration only, two wins at 25% requires eight opportunities. No close-rate estimate or 90-day lead guarantee is established here. Set targets after budget, capacity and baseline lead records are available.

Decision rules:

- Broken enquiry path: fix immediately before more promotion.
- Eligible pages not indexed: inspect the reason and current version; do not repeatedly submit unchanged URLs.
- Visits with unclear enquiries: examine page intent and actual buyer objections before adding traffic.
- Qualified leads without proposals/wins: examine response, fit, scope, credibility and pricing before changing SEO.
- Low relevant traffic at week 12: continue technical review and assess content/distribution quality; do not declare SEO disproven on this short, small sample.
- Unsustainable workload: cut channels and retain the strongest offer/proof combination.
- Regional pages: publish only when distinct usefulness and accurate service arrangements exist. No minimum word count or arbitrary publication spacing is treated as a Google rule.
